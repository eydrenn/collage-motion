# Direction: from an idea to a scene

The other pages say what each field does. This one says how to use them so the result is a video people watch to the end. It is advice, not a rule: follow the idea when it asks for something else.

## The shape of a piece

A piece in this style is a chain of small complete pictures, one idea each.

- **One idea per shot.** A shot shows one thing happening: someone arrives, something breaks, a number appears. When the idea changes, the shot changes.
- **One caption per shot**, a short phrase, not a sentence: about 22 letters fit on the caption line.
- **A background colour per shot.** The colour is the mood: navy for calm or night, red for alarm, yellow for an idea, moss or teal for neutral explanation. Change it at every tear, and never use the same colour twice in a row.
- **A title only when the shot opens a new part**: a time of day, a place, a chapter. Most shots have a caption and no title.

## Rhythm

Measured on two published videos made in this technique (56 and 67 seconds):

| | Video 1 | Video 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Shots | 19 | 16 |
| Typical shot (median) | 2.4 s | 3.3 s |
| Average shot | 2.9 s | 4.2 s |
| Cuts every 10 seconds | 3.2 | 2.3 |
| Longest shot | 13.6 s | 14.8 s |
| Loudness | -14.1 LUFS | -14.4 LUFS |

What to take from it:

- **Aim for shots of 2 to 4 seconds.** Shorter than 1.5 is a flash; longer than 5 needs something happening inside it.
- **One long shot is fine**, once or twice in a piece, when the caption is doing the talking. Give it a slow push and two or three actions.
- **The cuts follow the voice, not the music.** In both videos the cuts landed on the beat no more often than chance. Cut on the word.
- **Something changes about every second**: a letter landing, a face, a pose, a piece entering, the camera. A second with nothing changing is a frozen picture.

## Inside a shot

A shot of 3 seconds has room for about this:

1. The background and the pieces that are already there (0 s).
2. The caption arrives (0.3 to 0.8 s): the strip snaps in, the letters fly to it.
3. The event (1 to 1.5 s): a piece pops in, or a character changes pose and face.
4. The reaction (1.5 to 2.2 s): a second character answers, with a punch-in on the face.
5. A hold (the last half second), then the tear.

Keep two or three pieces and one or two characters per shot. A character fills a third to a half of the frame height; a reaction close-up fills more.

## Movement

- **Poses snap.** A pose is held, then it changes in one drawing. Use two or three pose changes per shot at most.
- **Change the face with the pose**, in the same action. A new pose with the old face reads as a mistake.
- **A slow push on every shot that holds** (`zoom: 1.08` to `1.15` over the whole shot).
- **A punch-in on the reaction** (`zoom: 1.4` to `1.6` in a quarter of a second, `on` the head), then cut back or tear.
- **A shake** when something lands or breaks, together with the piece's own `shake`.
- **Alternate two poses for a loop**: `dance-a` and `dance-b`, `look-up` and `look-down`, every 0.3 to 0.5 seconds.
- **Symbols carry the emotion**: `anger`, `drop`, `exclamation`, `question`, `sparkle`, `zzz`, popped in near the head, at the same instant as the face.

## Words on screen

- The caption is what the voice is saying, shortened, or the punchline the voice sets up. Never a full transcript.
- Capitals only (the tool does it). No full stop unless the stop is the joke.
- Use `strip: "black"` for the normal caption, `"cream"` on dark backgrounds, `"red"` or `"yellow"` for the line that matters.
- Ransom letters are for titles and for one shouted word. A whole caption in ransom letters is hard to read.
- Always `place`, never coordinates: `references/safe-zones.md`.

## Sound

- The built-in effects already follow the events. If a shot feels noisy, mute `letter` before anything else.
- With a narration, write the times as words: `references/voice.md`.
- Leave a quarter of a second of quiet before the last line.

## The order of work

1. Write the piece as a list of shots: for each one, the idea, the caption, the colour.
2. Still frames for the two or three shots that set the look. Show them.
3. The voice, if there is one, and its words file.
4. The scene, shot by shot, checking the contact sheet as you go.
5. The animatic, with the voice: fix the rhythm here.
6. The preview, then the final.

## Before showing

- Does every shot say one thing?
- Is there a zoom on every shot longer than two seconds?
- Does any shot repeat the colour of the one before it?
- Can each caption be read in the time it stays?
- Does the first second show the subject of the piece?
